# 📖 Inside the Case Study: Deconstructing a Corporate Blind Spot

Most corporate initiatives die in PowerPoint. 

They start with a massive press release, a grand executive promise, and a lot of goodwill. But when those promises hit the gears of everyday operations, they stall out. This project is a look inside that exact friction point, modeled during a strategy simulation with **Boston Consulting Group (BCG)**.

The client was "RetailCo," a major national retailer trying to solve a dual-mandate: increase their percentage of diverse suppliers (MWBEs) and safely deploy a **$25M strategic impact fund** into the community. 

Here is the story of how I took a chaotic mountain of unstructured data and built an operational bridge to make those goals reality.

---

## 🛠️ Act I: The Chaos of the 10-Week Blueprint
When a company pledges $25M, everyone wants to move fast. But speed without a map breeds scope creep. My first task was to force discipline onto the timeline by building a strict **Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)** across two parallel tracks:

* **The Procurement Track:** Forcing RetailCo to stop guessing and start tracking. I mapped out a timeline moving from baseline demographic audits (Weeks 1-3) to stakeholder standard rewrites (Weeks 4-7) and targeted regional pilots (Weeks 8-10).
* **The Governance Track:** Setting the rules for the $25M. This meant defining the target operating model, mapping out explicit accountability matrices, and setting up an aggressive due diligence pipeline to vet community funds before a single dollar left the building.

*The raw, time-phased dataset behind this master schedule lives right here in `project_controls/retailco_10week_master_schedule.csv`.*

---

## 📊 Act II: The Disconnect (What the Data Actually Said)
The real turning point happened when I was handed a messy brief full of raw quantitative survey metrics and unstructured interview transcripts from both corporate buyers and diverse business owners. 

Standard corporate reviews usually look at this data and declare a "pipeline problem"—claiming there aren't enough diverse businesses ready to scale. **The data told a completely different story.** It revealed a structural credit crunch:

* **The Letter of Intent Paradox:** **63% of diverse suppliers** admitted they lacked the working capital to compete. I uncovered notes showing that local banks routinely denied credit line expansions to these manufacturers—even when they had a signed purchase order from RetailCo in their hands.
* **The Scale Wall:** Because they couldn't get credit to buy raw materials, **only 38% of MWBE owners** felt confident they could meet RetailCo's massive, inflexible minimum order quantities.
* **The Procurement Blind Spot:** On the flip side, while **83% of corporate executives** praised diversity socially, **only 17% of retailers** had any active supplier financing mechanisms in place to help these vendors scale. Combine that with the fact that **94% of diverse owners** reported experiencing conscious or unconscious bias during traditional pitching cycles, and the initiative was practically engineered to stall.

---

## 🚀 Act III: Moving from Problems to Infrastructure
A strategy deck shouldn't just be an autopsy of what's broken; it needs to show the path out. 

I restructured the final deliverables around a **"Hurdle-to-Opportunity" Matrix** for the C-suite. The breakthrough was showing leadership that the **$25M capital fund** shouldn't be treated as a separate charity project. Instead, by investing it through a Community Development Financial Institution (CDFI), RetailCo could use that money to provide direct, low-interest credit lines to their own pipeline of diverse vendors. 

By funding the suppliers, they would solve the working capital crunch, allow the vendors to meet the high order minimums, and directly de-risk RetailCo’s own supply chain.

## 🏆 What This Project Proved
This portfolio isn't just a collection of files. It’s an exercise in structural empathy and data synthesis. It demonstrates the ability to:
1. **Slice Through Noise:** Turning emotional, messy qualitative interview text into systematic, addressable business pillars.
2. **Design Target Operating Models:** Structuring timelines and workflows where accountability is clear and risk is mitigated.
3. **Communicate Upstream:** Translating complex market realities into clean, executive-ready presentations (`executive_briefings/retailco_supplier_diversity_insights_deck.pdf`) that compel leadership to act.
